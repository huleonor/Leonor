# Leonor: rules for every session

The look is Lo-Fi (the user's choice in the 2026-11 redesign, anchor C of three made with the frontend-design skill, Ilm-Alan/frontend-design, and its anti-slop rules): a practice notebook, set out in DESIGN.md. It replaced Cartaz, the Swiss poster. DESIGN.md is the system to follow.

Read DESIGN.md before touching any UI. These rules are fixed unless the user changes them.

Deploys only when he says (the user's choice 2026-11): changes are committed and pushed to the session's branch, but no pull request into main is opened or merged until he asks for a deploy. A merge into main deploys the app on GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`, https://huleonor.github.io/Leonor/, built with `BASE_PATH=/Leonor/`, so every file the app fetches goes through `import.meta.env.BASE_URL`, never a bare `/path`). Vercel's deploys from git are off (`vercel.json`, to save its deployments); `api/plan.ts` lives only there, so on Pages the teaching plan comes from the rules.

## Brand (2026-11)
- The app is called Leonor. There is no logo in the app (the user found it ugly, 2026-11): the shell has none. The key mark (`public/brand/leonor-mark.svg`, `<Logo />`) stays only for the PNG icons in `public/icons` and the iPhone icon. There is no profile screen (the user removed it): the penguin avatar code (`Penguin.tsx`, `Profile.tsx`) stays unused.
- No game elements: no energy, gems, shop, XP, streaks, medals or daily goals, and no Premium or paywall. What is left is the music and his progress in it: a percent per song, parts learnt, and the stars on a practice result. Progress per song shows in the Library rows. The old economy code (`Economy.tsx`, `energy.ts`, `billing.ts`, `Paywall.tsx`) and the old unit path's screens (`PathHome.tsx`, `Sections.tsx`, `PathArt.tsx`, `TheoryLesson.tsx`) were deleted (the 2026-11 audit, P2); `path.ts` stays because the lessons use it, with its chests counted as passed.
- Three destinations (the 2026-11 audit, the user's yes; Home and the Watch tab are gone): a bottom tab bar with Song, Library and Settings (the gear). The first screen is Song (`Journey.tsx`; the user's "more professional", 2026-11): a quiet head ("Learning now" and Change song on one line, the title in Times sized to fit, composer and size in Courier), his progress as a bar of sections (one segment per section, as long as its bars, filled in ink by the videos watched and in teal once learnt, the next one edged pink; the percent on the marker and the next section beside it), then the video he was on as a still to pick up again (`ContinueCard` in `HomePage.tsx`, remembered per song; Practise beside it once that section is watched), Change song, and under a Sections heading the sections as one index card of rows in the order of the song, dashed rules between them (the 2026-11 audit, P2: a list, not a winding path; `SectionList` in `Journey.tsx`): each with its number (a tick once learnt), its name in the song's form, what it is in italic and where he is in a few Courier words; tapping the row opens its videos, two square icon buttons at its right: Hear opens it played through at real speed with the hands and no voice (`listenClip` in `reels.ts`, never counted as watched), Practise opens its lessons; the next row pink, Full song closing the card in ink; no stars. The Continue card is the one taped card (no shadow), and the overview reads "Overview of the song". It shows as soon as its own song is read (the catalog reads that song first, `catalog.ts`), a skeleton until then, never the add-the-file card for a song he has. The videos (Watch) open from the song (Continue, a section, a card) and take the whole screen, with a back arrow to the song at the top left always there (fainter while the video plays). Library: his songs; tapping one makes it the song being learnt. No profile tab.
- Two languages (Settings, Language; the user's choice 2026-11: everything, the voice too): English and Portuguese of Portugal. Every text on screen is written in both, side by side, with `L('English', 'Português')` (`src/lib/lang.ts`, the language set by App.tsx before anything renders; the app is remounted when it changes, and the catalog rebuilt so step names follow); the older dictionary keys (`t()` in `i18n.ts`) have their Portuguese too. The videos' words in Portuguese are written by hand like the English (`reelScriptsPt.ts`: every phase's words, `talk` and the Interstellar step tips) and the rules write their lines in both (`reels.ts`, `tr()`). Notes in the Portuguese videos follow Note names (Dó Ré Mi by default in Portuguese, set when he picks the language, or letters): the analysis stays in letters and `NT` names them as he reads them when the text is made, so a Portuguese line never starts with the words "A" or "E". The Portuguese voice is Kokoro's own Portuguese voice (`pf_dora`) reading European Portuguese phonemes from espeak-ng, recorded by the same `scripts/voice-bank.mjs` (keys carry their language; `apt-get install espeak-ng` first); a Portuguese line not recorded is said by the device's Portuguese voice (the model on the device reads only English). Hints, Mastery's plans and the course's step names are in both.
- Settings (`SettingsSheet.tsx`): Your piano (tapping the one that is on turns it off), Language, Note names, Finger numbers; Videos: Speed (slower, normal, faster: a factor on the planned pace), Wait for me (the video stops at a note he has not played), Hands on the keys (with the hand model's CC BY-SA credit), Natural voice; then Hear the keys I play and App plays the other hand. No practice reminder and no About card (the user's choice 2026-11); the iPhone app's update notice shows only when it is out of date. The piano is asked once (`PianoStep.tsx`, the 2026-11 audit): the first time he goes into the videos without a piano connected, one short step with the same choices as Settings (`PianoChoices`), Test your piano, a line saying the microphone only listens for his notes and nothing is recorded or sent, and Skip for now (Start the videos once one is on); never asked again.
- The skill `.claude/skills/mobile-app-ui-design` guides layout and UX; these rules win where they differ (Phosphor not Lucide, no emoji, our tokens and 10px unit).

## Concept (2026-11): one song at a time, taught by reels
- The app teaches one song at a time (`Journey.tsx`, the Song tab), Interstellar Experience by Tony Ann first. The song is cut into its phases, where the music changes (the user's choice: by phase, never by hand, and few of them), and each phase is named by its place in the song's form, as a musician says it (the user's choice 2026-11, from a sheet music app's section buttons: Intro, Verse 1, Interlude, Verse 2, Build, Climax, Outro for Interstellar Experience; written by hand with the script, `SECTIONS` in `reelScripts.ts`, with how the voice says each; a song without a script takes names written for the bars the rules cut it into, `RULE_SECTIONS` in `reelScripts.ts`, Sarabande, Passacaglia and Clair de Lune, kept only while those bars match, else Part 1, Part 2): on screen, in the voice ("Next up, verse 1") and in the words (a section, never a phase). The paused video shows the sections as a row of cards (`SectionRow.tsx`), each with a speaker to hear it on the app's piano (`listen.ts`, its bars at real speed, one at a time, marked as the app's for the mic) and its name to jump to its first video, the one he is on pink; the Song tab lists them (see Brand), and each phase is taught by a run of short vertical videos, TikTok style (`Reels.tsx`, `clipsOf` in `src/lib/reels.ts`; the user's choice 2026-11: more videos, slower, scroll down and copy; then, after the 2026-11 audit, a short series: at most twelve videos a section, `SERIES`, trimmed by priority, a second move, the hand-alone videos of later steps, a second jump, a second tour, a third pattern, then neighbouring both-hands steps merged up to six bars, then eight; Interstellar Experience went from 115 videos and two hours to 74 and 83 minutes): before everything the song itself (the user's choice 2026-11: "start with an overview of the song and how it is divided"): one video, its theme (`mainPart`) at real speed with the hands while the voice says what the song is, its sections in order and how each is learnt (patterns, small steps, the whole section), the section names as its title (its own phase, n 0, never part of a section's series); then in each section first the idea (the phase's opening heard once at its real speed while the voice explains it, and then says how the section will be learnt, "How we learn it: first its pattern for the right hand and one for the left, then six small steps, slowly, then the whole section.", counted from its own videos, and up to two tips read from its notes against the rest of the song: a jump, the hardest or one of the easier sections, hands apart, a wide reach, more black keys, the quickest part, repeats; the opening plays on as long as the voice speaks), then, before the first hand video of a step that brings a new figure, the pattern on its own (one beat of it, slowly, four times, then Train with me four times; the voice names its keys: the finger on each and where it sits from middle C, "First, just the pattern. E, C, A, C. Finger 3 on E. Thumb on A. It sits just above middle C."; the user's choice 2026-11: learn the patterns from the start, and say which keys). The phase's patterns come first, right after the idea (the user's choice 2026-11: "more focused on the patterns, explaining and repeating movements and patterns, that is where you really learn"): for each hand, each figure that comes back in the phase (up to three) gets the pattern video, then the same shape on each chord it lands on, one after the other (up to four chords, once each, "Now the same shape on each chord. E, C, A, C. ... Keep the hand in its shape and move it as one."), then the moves between those chords (up to two, the two chords back and forth three times, slower, the voice saying what stays and what moves: the whole hand, the top note staying while the others move, or only some fingers), each with two rounds of Train with me; then step by step, a step being a musical phrase (two bars at least, up to four until it holds a dozen notes and four seconds of music, fewer notes and at most two or three bars where it is hard; never one held note, the user's complaint 2026-11): the left hand alone, the right hand alone (slow enough for about 2.5 notes a second, never over 50%; each only when that hand plays a shape he has not met yet and has something to learn in it, six notes over three keys or more; a figure like top, middle, low, middle over a new chord, or a bar three quarters the same, counts as met), both hands (about 3 notes a second, never over 60%; where a simple hand, a held note or octave, is said instead of getting its own video), and at the end the whole phase at 75% (at most sixteen bars a video). Everything is gentler the harder the music is (the user's choice 2026-11, above all in the hard phases): `difficulty.ts` measures every step from its notes (speed, reach, leaps, black keys, chords, how independent the hands are, 0 to 1), and a hard step gets fewer notes a second (down to 60% of the target), a lower speed cap, each new hand on its own even with less in it, no merging with its neighbours, three rounds of Train with me instead of two; a hard phase runs at down to 55% in stretches of eight bars. `await window.__reelDifficulty('song-id')` in the dev console lists every video's difficulty, speed, rounds and length. The videos follow the piece's own difficulty (the user's choice 2026-11): the hard spot of a step, its biggest quick jump of a hand (a ninth or more), gets its own video before the hand video ("Watch the jump. From A, up to the high E. Look at the new key before you move.", the two keys four times, slowly; the same kind of jump once, at most two a phase), and a step with a jump goes slower. They follow him too (`ReelFeed`, from his piano in Train with me): a video played clean (at most one wait and one wrong key) skips the next step's hands-alone, pattern and jump videos, straight to both hands; a pace kept per song rises 10% with a clean video and falls 15% with a poor one (between 70% and 130% of the planned speeds, the idea always at its real speed); and a place he keeps missing (twice or more) gets a video made on the spot right after ("Let's fix this spot. You missed E. Here it is again, slowly.", the group with its neighbours, four times, slower). A discreet "Explain" chip (lightbulb, Courier, thin edge) at the top right of a video, a moment (five seconds) into it and only while it plays (the user's choice 2026-11), takes the video's music apart on the spot (`explainClips` in `reels.ts`): pieces of a bar (half a bar when it is busy; two bars or more of a long stretch, at most eight pieces), each hand alone and then both, at 70% of the video's speed, every piece its own short video with its notes named ("Let's take it apart, slower. Right hand. E, C, A, C."), put right after the video, and then the whole part again. Steps of shapes he knows, in a row, become one both-hands video up to six bars ("Same shapes as before, now on new chords: G, D"); the very same notes again get none. The voice never says bar numbers. Every video (after the idea) teaches in four beats, the user's choice (2026-11: "closer to teaching, a train with me part, pause and practise two more times"): the voice explains while the keys it names light; the music, slow; "Train with me": "Your turn, play it with me", a count of 1, 2, 3 (a soft click on each beat, the number big on screen; the user's choice 2026-11), then the notes start falling (no click after the 3, the user's choice), and the same music again with its notes drawn open, for him to play along, in rounds (the user's choice 2026-11: practising on his own was no use, he cannot remember the notes yet): two rounds for a step, three when it is hard, one for a pattern, a jump, a fix or the whole phase, and never more than about seventy seconds of music in all, so a long merged step takes one and no video runs past two minutes; each round has its line ("Once more. The piano plays softer.", "One last time. You lead."), its count-in and the notes falling, the kicker says "Train with me, 2 of 3", and the help fades each round (the piano guiding at 75%, 60%, 50% with his piano connected, always heard: at 10% it seemed the sound had gone, the user's complaint 2026-11; without a piano it stays at 85%) while the notes stay on screen; with a piano connected it waits for him (the user's choice: "the video stops while I don't play at the right time"): at a note group he has not played a quarter of a second after it should sound the video stops, the keys to play outlined and numbered and the kicker saying "Your turn: play E C", and it goes on the moment he plays them; after each round a card pops for a moment with how many note groups he played on time ("Round 1: 16 of 20 on time", on time meaning as it sounded or within half a second of the video stopping for it; a pop when it is 80% or more). Then it says what comes next ("Nice. Next up, the right hand, bars 1 and 2. Swipe up."), so the videos read as one lesson, and loops Train with me (without the voice) until he scrolls on. There is no practise-alone stop any more. Sound effects on visible actions, few and exact (`public/sfx`, Pixabay licence, from the HyperFrames skill): a whoosh as a video's title arrives, a click on each count-in beat, a pop for each run counted, a riser into an impact as the whole phase starts. Nothing plays at full speed except the idea. "Practise" beside the video opens the phase's normal lessons. The Song tab is one card per section (its videos watched, its practice) and then the full song.
- A reel is never a recorded video: it is drawn live from the song's own notes, the same way as the Für Elise TikToks made with HyperFrames (the user's choice, 2026-11: "the app's videos like these, made the same way"): the step's kicker small and its notes huge at the top (teal for a left-hand step), one keyboard for both hands (cut in two, each hand's keys side by side with a gap, when the hands are far apart, so the keys stay big enough to read) with the notes falling onto it in the hand's colour with their letter, keys outlined and numbered as the voice names them, a key that sounds filled with its letter and finger and a short flash rising from it, the caption under the keyboard, nothing else (no bass cells, no beat-by-beat row, no big bar number), with the real piano sound, first slowly, then at full speed, then "Your turn". The picture has life from the notes (the user's choice 2026-11): the camera follows the hand without shaking (the user found it trembling): the video is cut in blocks of 2.4 s, each framing its own notes, and the camera glides from one block's frame to the next over the last 0.8 s, then holds still (never tighter than six tenths of the video's frame; on a keyboard cut in two over a long stretch, a half with more than eleven white keys, each half follows its own hand the same way, at least nine white keys, the screen shared out by what each shows; the keyboard's height is set from the video's own frame so it never pumps); no dashed lines through the notes (the user's choice); a jump video draws an arc with a head over the keys from the key left to the key landed on; and the keyboard counts the beat (1 2 3 4 at the left above the keys, the beat it is on on the marker) while the music plays. The hands of the voice that teaches are real human hands in 3D (the user's choice 2026-11, after the cartoon gloves looked strange): only the hands are 3D, the video stays flat (`Hands3D.tsx`, three.js, loaded on its own): "Rigged hand" by Elena FF (Sketchfab, CC BY-SA 4.0, `public/models/hand` with its licence, credited in Settings), a skinned hand with palm volume and a bone for every phalanx, mirrored for the left. They are drawn like a comic (the user's choice 2026-11, from a piano video he liked): flat skin in three tones from one light (`--hand-skin`, `--hand-shade`, `--hand-deep`, a toon ramp read in colour) and an ink line round each finger (`--hand-line`, three's OutlineEffect); the fingers lie long and relaxed over the keys, a free finger a key from its neighbour (never splayed wider than its knuckles) and one that cannot reach its key stays in its lane. They land on the keys: a hand's positions move with the camera as it glides (they live in screen pixels), the wrist follows the notes about to sound, the hand moves in towards the black keys when a finger needs them, a finger about to play takes its key directly instead of its lagging spring, and where a tip must hit a key the miss is measured on the posed hand and folded back into the target twice. The knuckles span about four white keys (more in a video whose hand stretches past an octave), the little finger and thumb spread wide, so the thumb reaches an octave; on a wide screen with the hands on, the video shows enough keys (about one per tenth of its height) for a hand of real proportions to fit under them, also when the keyboard is cut in two. A black key is pressed smoothly and visibly: the finger glides into the black keys as its note comes, lifts a little higher to clear them, strikes the key's top and sinks in by its depth, and the hand eases forward on a critically damped spring and gives a little from the wrist with each press. A hand with nothing near to play waits over its next notes, never on the other hand. Seen from above as he sees his own hands, on a see-through canvas over the flat keys with the keys' plane mapped one to one onto their pixels (`KeysGeom`). The motion is solved, not keyframed: each finger has abduction within its range from a natural spread and three flexing joints (the end joint coupled to the middle one), a damped IK warm-started each frame so it keeps a soft curve, each bone turned from its rest pose to aim at the next joint; the thumb bends turned in towards the palm; the press follows the note (a lift just before, a quick strike on time, held while it sounds, an eased release), idle fingers hover at their own heights and dip with a pressing neighbour; the knuckles sit above the keys in front of them so the fingers arch; the hand travels on stiff, critically damped springs (stepped finely so a slow frame never throws it off), led by the notes about to sound (notes already held weigh little once a new one is coming, almost nothing past five keys, as the hand leaves a key on a leap and the pedal holds it), the wrist already leaning on the notes of the next quarter second so it has arrived when they sound (the user found it moving after the notes, 2026-11); the wrist turns into the movement, rolls towards the pressing finger and breathes; each finger has its own stiffness; while the voice names a key the hand points at it with the index finger; flat gloves where WebGL is missing; a Hands button beside the video turns them off. Their motion is judged by numbers (the user's choice 2026-11): `await window.__handCheck(20)` in the dev console (`src/lib/handCheck.ts`, our own measures, no other project's code or data) records the video on screen and reports per hand whether each tip lands on its key as the note sounds, how early it is there, whether it is down, the wrist's speed, wobble and jerk in keys, crowded fingers and fingers inside the keys while idle; run it on a few videos before and after any change to the hands. Only the video on screen draws its hands (the feed's neighbours stay still). His own keys show at every moment of a video, even while the voice explains (the user's choice 2026-11): a key he presses lights green when right and red when wrong, and a small mark with its letter rises from it in the same colour; right means a key of a note group due about then (half a second either way) in Train with me, and any key the video teaches otherwise; the meta line counts his keys ("Your keys: 12 right, 2 wrong"). A piano chip (key icon, red dot, green when connected) at the top right of every video opens Settings to connect it. Swipe up for the next video; tap to pause. The videos are printed on paper (the user found the full blue too strong): ink text, the right hand in riso pink, the left in teal. A voice explains it (`src/lib/voice.ts`): a natural voice recorded ahead of time, Kokoro-82M (`af_heart`) run in a session by `scripts/voice-bank.mjs` into `public/voice` (listed in `voiceBank.json`, with each piece's length), played whole or from its pieces (`voicePieces.ts`: the words around a list of notes, each note name recorded mid-list and at the end), so it is natural on any phone from the first video (on the device Kokoro took seconds a line and the robotic system voice filled in, the user's complaint 2026-11). The app's own songs are recorded line by line; a song he brings only as note-free sentences, its hand-written `talk` and the pieces. After changing any line, re-run the recorder (the skill says how). A line not recorded is made on the device by the same model in a worker (`kokoroWorker.ts`); the device's speech engine speaks only with the natural voice switched off. No server, no API key. It reads each video's line: for the first stretch of a phase the hand-written `talk` lines (the idea, each hand, both, the send-off), after that lines written by the rules from the notes, the way a teacher says them (a figure named once with the note that moves, a melody note by note), the piano steps back a little under it, and the caption shows what it says a few words at a time with the word being said lit, floating at the top of the falling notes (never under the keyboard, where it pushed the keys up; the user's choice 2026-11), the words that carry the lesson (a note, a finger, a shape, an action) set big and the little words small (from the voice's word marks, or the clock). The timeline gives each line room (`estimate`); there are no full-screen hand-name cards between passes, only a short breath while the notes already fall in. A Voice button beside the reel turns it off; the captions stay. The videos follow the HyperFrames production craft (the user's choice, 2026-11; DESIGN.md "Video craft"): everything drawn from the clock (scrub along the bottom edge), keys lit and numbered as the voice names them, a camera that pushes in on the video's keys over the first second, expo arrivals and short exits, titles and captions blurring in word by word. Only the craft is used: nothing is rendered to MP4, because his MIDI never leaves his device.
- Every video of Interstellar Experience after a phase's first step has its own hand-written tip (`STEP_TIPS` in `reelScripts.ts`, `steps` in a phase, keyed by kind L, R, B or J and the bar its step starts on): the trick of that moment as a teacher says it ("Keep the top finger where it is. The lower fingers step down."), said after the video's own line, never the notes read out; recorded whole with the rest.
- The phases and their words (title, hook, tricks, a line for each hand, captions by bar, a tip, and what the voice says, `talk`) are written by hand, by Claude in a session, after reading the score bar by bar, and shipped in `src/lib/reelScripts.ts` (limits and checks in `reelShape.ts`); no model and no API key at runtime. A script fits a song by the reading's hash (`version`, for the MIDI files shipped with the app) or, for a song he brings as his own MIDI, by the bass at fingerprint bars of the printed score (70% must match). A song without a script, or notes that do not match, gets its course parts grouped into at most seven phases and words the rules write from the notes. To write one, `await window.__reelFacts('song-id')` in the dev console gives every bar's bass and right hand and the score as text. Note letters follow the key (sharps, flats in a flat key). Scripts exist for Gymnopédie No. 1 (6 phases), Für Elise (2) and Interstellar Experience (7, read from the MuseScore score: 82 bars, 4/4, crotchet 99, two bars of 3/4 at 57-58). Never turn a copyrighted score into a MIDI file for him: he brings the MIDI. Repairing the MIDI he brings is fine (re-barring a converter's timing, dropping clef or time-signature signs it read as notes, putting a misread clef back, applying octave lines it ignored, reusing a bar of his own file where the score repeats it), and so is filling the few bars a converter lost, read from his own copy of the score, when he asks; transcribing a whole score is not. The repair script lives in the session scratchpad, never in the repo. His repaired Interstellar Experience file never goes in the repo.
- A hand's stretch in a phase video starts on the first bar where that hand plays.
- How to write and time these videos: the project skill `.claude/skills/piano-lesson-video` (adapted from an explainer-video guide: the series, the Show, Train, Check, Next formula, voice lines, pacing, sound, mistakes). A script also fits a MIDI he added as a new song (not into its slot) when its notes match the printed score's fingerprint.
- Time-signature changes are kept (`song.meter`, read from the MIDI): every bar's start and length come from the bar grid (`src/lib/bars.ts`), used by the parts, reels, reading, Mastery, the engine's bar lines and count-in, the metronome (each bar ticks in its own metre, beat 1 accented) and the score (an inline `[M:3/4]` where the metre changes). Never compute a bar as `ppq * 4 * num / den` from `song.timeSig` alone.
- Interstellar Experience is under copyright: it is a slot that takes his own MIDI file, like the other film and modern pieces; nothing of it ships with the app. It is always in his library (the user's choice): until his file is in, the Library shows it as a pink card asking for the MIDI file.

## Design system (Lo-Fi, a practice notebook; DESIGN.md has the detail)
- Colours only through the tokens in `src/styles.css` `:root`. Never hardcode a colour in a component or rule; add a token. A canvas reads the tokens with `getComputedStyle` on its own element. There is one theme, light.
- A paper-yellow page (`--paper` #EDE4CF, never cream; wells `--paper-2`), index cards in `--white` with a 2px ink edge (`--ink`, `--ink-2`; `--muted` only for hints and labels), dashed rules (`--rule-2`). Two riso inks: pink (`--pink`, text `--pink-text`, tint `--pink-soft`) and teal (`--teal`, `--teal-text`, `--teal-soft`); the yellow marker (`--marker`) highlights the current tab, the word being said, the next key and the percent; tape (`--tape`) holds down the one current card. `--field` is now the highlighted card (pink-soft with ink). One red (`--mark`) for a wrong note, one green (`--ok`) for a right note or a good run. No gradients, glows, blur or glass: the only radial gradients are halftone dot tiles, the only shadow a hard 3px pink block behind the primary button. Type is plain ink, never misregistered (the user found the riso offset on titles uncomfortable to read, 2026-11; `--riso` is none); a big head in words (both hands, the idea) is set in Times, smaller, and only note letters are huge.
- Hands: the right hand is riso pink, the left riso teal, everywhere (videos, falling notes, keys). A falling note is printed with the other ink's plate a few pixels off register under it.
- One primary style: ink fill with paper text and a 3px pink offset block. Default buttons are white cards with a 2px ink edge, turned a little; quiet ones are Courier words with a wavy pink underline. Sentence case, never uppercase, never tracked.
- Three system faces, no web font: Times 700 (`--font-display`) for titles, song and phase names and the captions (italic on the videos); Helvetica (`--font`) for words at 16px and, at 900, the huge note letters and the count-in; Courier 700 (`--font-mono`) for labels, meta lines, tab labels, numbers and percents.
- Square corners everywhere; circles only for small dots. Structure from index cards turned 0.5 to 2 degrees off the grid (by `nth-child`), never all square; halftone dots print only the covers (the user found them uncomfortable on the Library cards, 2026-11).
- Spacing on a 10px unit (10, 20, 30, 40).
- Motion is state-driven (`src/components/Motion.tsx`, the `motion` library): every animation answers a change of state, never decoration (a bar number sliding in, a percent bumping, a verdict popping, sheets rising, the help keyboard rising and falling). CSS transitions step like stop-motion (`steps(3)`, 240ms). No loops, no confetti. The app runs inside `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">`.
- Focus: 2px solid pink outline, 2px offset.

## Product UI rules (from the anti-slop skill)
- Icons: Phosphor only (`@phosphor-icons/react`), bold weight, fill for solid marks. Never hand-draw SVG icon paths; add the glyph to `Icons.tsx` or `GameIcons.tsx`.
- Zero em dashes and en dashes in anything the user sees. Use a hyphen, comma, colon or two sentences.
- Body text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1) on its ground. Paper text on the red or the green only at 15px and up or in display type.
- Screenshot phone and desktop before committing UI changes.
- No gradients, no purple, no decorative dots, no emoji, no middle dots joining text (a comma or a full stop instead), no eyebrows in capitals.
- One verb per action across the app. Button labels stay on one line.
- Loading, empty and error states for anything that loads.
- No logo in the app (see Brand). The penguin avatar code (`Penguin.tsx`) stays unused. No characters or mascots and no icon-on-a-disc illustrations: the picture of a song is its own opening on a staff (`<OpeningStaff />`, `SongCover.tsx`). Never use Duolingo's owl, logo or name in the app.
- The difficulty adapts invisibly: never show levels or "made easier" notes to the learner. Songs show no difficulty either.
- Step-by-step lessons (path lessons and a song's parts) are Simply Piano's page, the user's choice: a white page (`--white`), the staff big across it (the grand staff about half the stage's height; a short step grows to fill the width, a long one slides, never less than a bar on screen), a pink now line, the help keyboard a white strip along the bottom edge: the bar holds only pause (left) and the n/N step pill with its arrows and rep dots (right), sized like Simply's (44px pause, 34px counter and arrows, all square); no step name, chips or piano icon on it (the step name is for screen readers). The staff is Simply Piano's: its lines run on across the whole screen, the clef and key signature stay at the left edge, played notes fade out behind them, and it grows with the screen. Nothing moves before he plays: the cursor starts on the first note, no run-up.
- The keyboard is help so he learns to read: never there from the start (the coach can never set it to "right away"; a stall counts only after his first note), hidden until he plays a wrong note or stalls, then a compact strip with every key the piece needs (next key yellow, `--key-next`) rises along the bottom and sinks again once he plays right. Options can keep it on.
- Every level teaches Simply Piano's way: free play first (the music waits for each note, no rhythm; hand by hand for two hands), then the same music "With the beat" with the metronome ticking (count-in, accent on beat 1). The metronome can be switched off in the options.
- Playing a whole song is minimal: close, progress bar, the score on a card, Listen, Learn step by step and Mastery below, options under a gear. There the keyboard is help, not furniture: it rises only when he is stuck or plays a wrong note, shows a short stretch of keys with the right ones lit, and goes away again. "Play the first note to start" shows until he does.
- Life comes from state: praise popping at milestones (5, 10, 15, 20, 30 right in a row), the step pill springing forward, a step's verdict popping green or red. No streak chip.
- The piano connection is never a banner: a small piano-keys icon with a red dot (green when connected) in the game bar opens Settings. Lessons do not show it: setting up the keyboard lives in Settings.
- The tabs are a bottom tab bar, like an app's (the user found the old navigation not app-like, 2026-11): Song, Library, Settings, each a Phosphor bold glyph over a Courier label, the current one's glyph filled and its label on the marker. In the videos the bar hides and a back arrow at the top left returns to the song. On a wide screen the app is one 560px page column on the dotted desk, except Watch, which opens to the whole width.
- A video's actions (Practise, Again, Voice, Speed (slower, normal, faster, the same setting as Settings, applied at once from the video's start), Hands; play is the big button in the middle and the next video a swipe) show only while it is paused, sliding in on the right edge, so nothing covers the video while it plays (the user's choice); the piano chip and the meta line (section and video) show only while it is paused too (the back arrow stays, fainter), and the head is small (the notes at most 56px). His key count stays while he plays. In a short landscape window the actions' labels sit beside their icons in a column under the piano chip, and nothing on the video is under 14px (kicker 16px). Wider than tall (a phone on its side, a computer), the video is horizontal: the head in one line along the top (kicker, notes, where), the keyboard across the whole width under it, the caption under the keys (the user: easier to see; a column of words on the left left it empty).
- The old unit path is gone from the app (its screens deleted, the 2026-11 audit); `path.ts` remains only for the lessons that use it.
- Mastery checks (`mastery.ts`): after 100% a check falls due after a week, then two weeks, then a month: the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, any day from the due date, with a week's grace. When one is due it is the next Mastery section. Three kept and the song is Forever (no more checks). A check missed past the grace drops it to silver until he plays it clean again, which brings gold back and starts the checks over; nothing is lost for good.
- Song covers (`SongCover.tsx`) are one design for every piece, never photos: a tile in one of the `--cover-*` colours with a staff across it carrying the song's own opening (the first notes of its melody, in white, at their pitch); a big white Phosphor glyph stands in when the notes are not loaded (a slot waiting for its MIDI). The app's songs take the colours in turn; an imported MIDI gets one by hash.
- Songs: public-domain pieces ship with the app (Gymnopédie No. 1, the opening of Für Elise written by `scripts/fur-elise.mjs`, Handel's Sarabande and Passacaglia, Clair de Lune); every song the app has ready is in his library by itself (the user's choice 2026-11: the pieces it ships, a slot filled with his MIDI file, the files he uploaded). Film and modern pieces under copyright are slots that take his own MIDI file, listed under Add songs with their composer, work and year, a line on what it is like and "Bring your own MIDI file". The Library (`Songs.tsx`) shows each song as a plain white index card: its main part (`mainPart` in `preview.ts`, the stretch of bars that comes back most, eight to fourteen seconds) as notes falling onto a strip of keys (`SongPreview.tsx`), its title, composer and percent (or the length and "not started"); the song learnt now is the big taped card on top. With a mouse the preview plays and sounds while the pointer rests on a card and a click opens it; with a finger the first tap plays it ("Tap again to learn it") and the second opens it.
- Mastery plays like Simply Piano: a section (a part of the song) is a run of steps, phrase by phrase (about four bars, never under two), each repeated until it has its good runs (the dots under the step count turn into green ticks; once in free play, twice with the beat), so he keeps moving through the song. A part is learnt enough to go on to the next once he has played it with the beat, clean at any speed, nearly right, or twice and more than half right; the song keeps moving and what is still weak comes back later (each part faster, warm-ups, drills), so he feels it growing. After each run only a tick or "again" shows, then the same step, the next one, or a smaller stretch where it went wrong: no screens and no taps. With the beat the count-in starts by itself. The result card shows only at the end of a section, for three seconds, then the next section starts on its own (unless it suggests stopping for the day). The lesson staff is drawn like Simply's: a white page (`--staff-ground`), thin grey staff and bar lines (`--staff-line`), the two staves one staff-height apart (`%%sysstaffsep 20`), black notes, green once played, red when wrong, finger numbers in bold Helvetica that stay black (above the note for the right hand, below it for the left), no time signature, and no cursor in free play (the now line shows only with the beat); about four bars across and as big as the screen's height allows. A step that fits on the screen stays still and centred and the now line moves over it; a longer one slides under the now line.
- Mastery (`src/lib/mastery.ts`) plans a song's lessons live from every note he plays: a strength per bar, per hand, per hands-together and per movement (leaps, jumps to a chord, chord changes, new positions). Notes first in free play, then a tempo ladder (60% to 100%) climbed after two good runs; a good run is 90% right with at most one slip. A stretch that goes wrong twice in one place gets that place (two bars, one hand if only one slipped) put in front of it; three misses with the beat slow it down (two when the model gives another try under 50%); four and it is left for another day. A step the model gives under 60% gets one lead-in played once just before it (the same music a rung slower, its harder half, or the harder hand alone, whichever is closest to the target and at least 75%), at most two per section; a free-play step that fails twice with no smaller stretch to zoom into gets the same lead-in. Forgetting is learnt too (`src/lib/memory.ts`): every bar, hands-together bar and movement has its own half-life, moved after each return following a gap by how much was left (a clean return makes it sturdier), and new things start from his typical half-life; review comes just before it fades (85% left). It suggests stopping when his playing levels off. The meter follows him as he plays: every bar fills from the notes (each hand alone, then both hands) and then the beat, rung by rung, and even a run that did not pass counts for half of what it got right, so it never sits at 0% while he is learning; only the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, on two different days makes 100%. The AI runs on the device: a small prediction model (`src/lib/predict.ts`, a logistic regression), learnt from his own notes, estimates how likely he is to play each possible exercise well and picks the next one near 80-85% (the zone of ideal effort). Exercises are always cut from the song's real notes, never invented: a weak movement gets its own drill, the same move taken from up to four places in the song where he plays it worst (the chord before and the chord it moves to, each kept on its own beat, at least three places, played one after the other), and what he plays there counts for those bars and that move; he never sees the analysis or chooses the exercise, he just gets the next one.
- Each song has a teaching plan (`src/lib/strategy.ts`, shape in `strategyShape.ts`): Claude reads the score (`reading.ts`: every bar's notes per hand, the parts, the repeated shapes, measured density and how often the hands land together) through `api/plan.ts` (Vercel, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; `GROQ_API_KEY` for testing, its plans asked again each time the app opens) and decides the fastest way in: the order of the parts, real musical phrases, whether each part goes hand by hand per phrase, both hands from the start, or the lead hand through the whole part first, patterns that come back everywhere to learn on their own first, hard spots to drill as soon as their part starts, and how slow to start with the beat. It is asked once per song and version and kept; until it answers, or without a key, rules measured from the notes write the same kind of plan. The plan is checked against the song before it is used, and Mastery follows it while still adapting live to every note he plays. He never sees the plan.
- Hints come from an offline bank, keyed by the kind of mistake (a neighbour key, an overshot leap, a stall before reading, early or late with the beat, hands not together). An LLM may draft the bank; it is reviewed by hand and shipped inside the app. A hint is one short line in the moment after a run, at most one at a time, never interrupting play: only after a run that did not count, never two runs in a row, each kind at most twice a section (`src/lib/hints.ts`: the diagnosis and the bank).
- Finger numbers fade through Mastery: on every note while he learns the notes (a hand alone, both hands, a weak spot), only where the hand moves once he plays the part with the beat, none when he speeds a learnt part up or plays the whole song.
- With the beat in Mastery, while he is new to it (60% and 70%, and less at 80%), the music waits a moment at a note he has not played (1.5 s, 1 s, 0.5 s) instead of marking it missed, and a slip never sends him back; from 90%, and in the whole song, reviews and checks, it is played for real.
- With the beat, every count-in is 1, 2, 3 in tempo (the user's choice 2026-11, as in the videos), ticking on the beat and the music starts on beat 1 right after it, so he feels where the first note lands. A note counts from 0.3 s early until the now line has passed it (half its length, 0.3 to 0.6 s late); a note played on the last count is on time, and the right key a little outside that window is not a wrong note. A slip (wrong or missed note) takes him back to beat 1 of the bar before it, after the same count-in; past four slips the run stops.
- The microphone is honest and fits the room: a round of Train with me in which no key at all came from his piano says so ("I did not hear your piano. Bring the phone closer or play a little louder.", or for a cable or Bluetooth that no notes came and to check it), never "0 of 20 on time"; Test your piano has a short calibration with the microphone on (`MicCalibration` in `InputCheck.tsx`): a moment of quiet, then any C softly three times, less sensitive if the quiet was heard, more sensitive and again (three tries) if Cs were missed, kept on the device (`micTuning` in `input.ts`), with Reset.
- The microphone never takes the app's own piano for his playing: every note a video or a lesson plays is marked as the app's while it sounds (`markAppPlayed`), and a key heard by the mic goes dark again on its own.
- The sound never stays off (`audio.ts`): the audio context is resumed whenever iOS suspends or interrupts it (screen lock, a call, the background), on the next touch or key, and checked every two seconds.
- Accidental touches are never mistakes (`engine.ts`): a wrong key from a keyboard waits 90ms before it counts. A ghost touch (very soft), the key bouncing, a neighbour brushed with the right key, or a key grazed and let go at once is left out (shown as "accidental touches not counted"); several wrong keys at once are one slip.
- The course adapts by topic, not by lesson count (`path.ts`): each unit asks for its skills to reach a goal that rises section by section. Short of it at the unit test, an "Extra practice" lesson made from his weakest of those skills appears (at most three); comfortably past it, the unit's "Personal practice" is not needed. Inside a lesson a perfect free-play run passes the free-play steps it proves (both hands prove each hand) and goes on to the beat; a fast learner starts with both hands and goes back to one at a time if that is hard. No test-outs to skip lessons.
- Free play is the follow mode: the cursor jumps to the next notes and waits, no bars or rests to sit through. Most steps are free; only the last steps of a part or level go "With the beat".
- The MIDI reader writes clean notation: accompaniment chords found in the right-hand track go to the left hand, a chord wider than a tenth in one hand gives its far notes (past a ninth from its own end) to the other hand when that one strikes nothing then (`shareWideChords` in `midi.ts`; Clair de Lune's right hand went from 99 such chords to 39), and slightly short performed notes snap to plain note values.
