# Hugo's Piano: rules for every session

The anti-slop skill is guidance for avoiding generic AI design, not a theme: the look stays Duolingo's light app.

Read DESIGN.md before touching any UI. These rules are fixed unless the user changes them.

## Design system (Duolingo's app look)
- Colours only through the tokens in `src/styles.css` `:root`. Never hardcode a colour in a component or rule; add a token. The app is always white, like Duolingo: there is no dark theme.
- Light and white like the Duolingo app: white ground, cards with a 2px `#e5e5e5` border and no drop shadow, `#f7f7f7` for wells.
- Primary is the logo's blue `#2b93ba` (edge `#1f7395`) with white bold uppercase text on chunky buttons (4px darker edge that presses down). It is also the one interactive accent (text links use `--blue-text`, underlined). Green (`--ok`, `#58cc02`) is only for a right answer or note; red only for a wrong one.
- Text `#3c3c3c`; muted `#777777` only for hints and labels, never body copy.
- Game colours (energy pink, gem blue, flame orange, star gold) each have a `-text` shade for text; use it.
- Unit colours carry their own text colour (`--unit-ink`): white, dark brown on gold.
- One family: Nunito, bold (body 700, headings 900, 0.8px tracking). Hierarchy by weight, modest sizes (page titles 22px).
- One radius: 12px for buttons, cards, inputs, dialogs. Circles for nodes and dots, full pills for chips.
- Spacing on a 10px unit (10, 20, 30, 40).
- Motion is state-driven (`src/components/Motion.tsx`, the `motion` library): every animation answers a change of state, never decoration. Springs for feedback (answer footer sliding up, a right option popping, a wrong one shaking, numbers bumping when they change, the progress bar springing forward, sheets rising, the help keyboard rising and falling). CSS transitions 300ms ease for hovers and colours. Loops only where they mean something (the START bubble, a ready chest). The app runs inside `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">`.
- Focus: 2px solid accent outline, 2px offset.

## Product UI rules (from the anti-slop skill)
- Icons: Phosphor only (`@phosphor-icons/react`), bold weight, fill for solid marks. Never hand-draw SVG icon paths; add the glyph to `Icons.tsx` or `GameIcons.tsx`.
- Zero em dashes and en dashes in anything the user sees. Use a hyphen, comma, colon or two sentences.
- Body text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1). Bold white labels on Duolingo's game colours are the accepted exception, as in Duolingo itself; never put small regular-weight text on them.
- Screenshot phone and desktop before committing UI changes.
- No AI-purple gradients, no decorative dots, no emoji, at most one middle dot per line.
- One verb per action across the app. Button labels stay on one line.
- Loading, empty and error states for anything that loads.
- The logo is the penguin with round glasses (`public/icons/logo.png`, `<Logo />`). The only character is his own penguin avatar (`Penguin.tsx`, drawn from plain circles and ellipses in Duolingo's geometric style, colours from the `--av-*` tokens): he picks its background, feathers and what it wears (glasses, headphones, beanie, bow tie, crown) on his profile, and it is the Profile tab's mark. No other characters or mascots; illustrations are big Phosphor icons on a tinted disc (`<Spot />`). Never use Duolingo's owl, logo or name in the app.
- The difficulty adapts invisibly: never show levels or "made easier" notes to the learner. Songs show no difficulty either.
- Step-by-step lessons (path lessons and a song's parts) keep Simply Piano's clear page in Duolingo's colours: the bar holds only pause (left) and the n/N step pill with its arrows and rep dots (right), sized like Simply's (44px pause, 34px pill and arrows); no step name, chips or piano icon on it (the step name is for screen readers). The staff is Simply Piano's: its lines run on across the whole screen, the clef and key signature stay at the left edge, played notes fade out behind them, and it grows with the screen. Nothing moves before he plays: the cursor starts on the first note, no run-up.
- The keyboard is help so he learns to read: never there from the start (the coach can never set it to "right away"; a stall counts only after his first note), hidden until he plays a wrong note or stalls, then a compact strip with every key the piece needs (next key gold) rises along the bottom and sinks again once he plays right. Options can keep it on.
- Every level teaches Simply Piano's way: free play first (the music waits for each note, no rhythm; hand by hand for two hands), then the same music "With the beat" with the metronome ticking (count-in, accent on beat 1). The metronome can be switched off in the options.
- Playing a whole song is minimal: close, progress bar, the score on a card, Listen, Learn step by step and Mastery below, options under a gear. There the keyboard is help, not furniture: it rises only when he is stuck or plays a wrong note, shows a short stretch of keys with the right ones lit, and goes away again. "Play the first note to start" shows until he does.
- Life comes from state, Duolingo style: a streak chip that counts right notes, praise popping at milestones (5, 10, 15, 20, 30 in a row), the step pill springing forward, a step's verdict popping green or red.
- The piano connection is never a banner: a small piano-keys icon with an orange dot (green when connected) in the game bar opens Settings. Lessons do not show it: setting up the keyboard lives in Settings.
- Tabs: Learn (the path), Songs, Shop, Profile. There is no Practice or Progress tab. Tab marks are solid Phosphor glyphs, each in its own colour (`--tab-learn`, `--tab-songs`, gem blue, `--tab-settings`), so each tab is known by shape and colour.
- The path has life, Duolingo style but without characters: every unit has a picture beside the trail (a big Phosphor glyph on a tinted disc in the unit's colour, with notes around it, `PathArt.tsx`), grey until the unit opens; nodes ahead keep their own icon in grey (`--locked-ink`) instead of a lock; finished lessons show their stars under the node. Tapping the unit banner (or a section divider) opens the Sections page (`Sections.tsx`): a card per section with its picture on a staff, what it teaches, units done, and Continue or Review; later sections show locked. No jumping ahead.
- Profile (`Profile.tsx`), Duolingo's layout: his penguin on a header in its background colour (edit button top right), his name and "Learning piano since", counts (songs, mastered, lessons), an Overview of four stat cards (day streak, total XP, best streak, songs mastered), and the Mastery collection: a medal per song in his library, the cover in a disc whose colour is its tier. Bronze at 50% mastery, silver at 75%, gold at 100%, Forever once gold is kept through three mastery checks. A medal that went up a tier since he last looked shines once.
- Mastery checks (`mastery.ts`): after 100% a check falls due after a week, then two weeks, then a month: the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, any day from the due date, with a week's grace. When one is due it is the next Mastery section. Three kept and the song is Forever (no more checks). A check missed past the grace drops it to silver until he plays it clean again, which brings gold back and starts the checks over; nothing is lost for good.
- Song covers (`SongCover.tsx`) are one design for every piece, never photos: a tile in one of the `--cover-*` colours with a staff across it and a big white Phosphor glyph, both picked from the song so it always looks the same; an imported MIDI gets one too.
- Songs: for now the app teaches one piece, Gymnopédie No. 1 (Satie), and teaches it well; it is in the library from the start. The Songs tab shows only his library (small square covers); the marketplace adds songs, and he can upload his own MIDI.
- Mastery plays like Simply Piano: a section (a part of the song) is a run of steps, phrase by phrase (about four bars, never under two), each repeated until it has its good runs (the dots under the step count turn into green ticks; once in free play, twice with the beat), so he keeps moving through the song. A part is learnt enough to go on to the next once he has played it with the beat, clean at any speed or nearly right; getting each part up to full speed comes after. After each run only a tick or "again" shows, then the same step, the next one, or a smaller stretch where it went wrong: no screens and no taps. With the beat the count-in starts by itself. The result card shows only at the end of a section, for three seconds, then the next section starts on its own (unless it suggests stopping for the day). The lesson staff is drawn like Simply's: a warm light ground (`--staff-ground`), thin grey staff and bar lines (`--staff-line`), the two staves one staff-height apart (`%%sysstaffsep 20`), black notes, teal once played, red when wrong, finger numbers in bold Nunito that stay black (above the note for the right hand, below it for the left), no time signature, and no cursor in free play (the now line shows only with the beat); about four bars across and as big as the screen's height allows. A step that fits on the screen stays still and centred and the now line moves over it; a longer one slides under the now line.
- Mastery (`src/lib/mastery.ts`) plans a song's lessons live from every note he plays: a strength per bar, per hand, per hands-together and per movement (leaps, jumps to a chord, chord changes, new positions). Notes first in free play, then a tempo ladder (60% to 100%) climbed after two good runs; a good run is 90% right with at most one slip. A stretch that goes wrong twice in one place gets that place (two bars, one hand if only one slipped) put in front of it; three misses with the beat slow it down (two when the model gives another try under 50%); four and it is left for another day. A step the model gives under 60% gets one lead-in played once just before it (the same music a rung slower, its harder half, or the harder hand alone, whichever is closest to the target and at least 75%), at most two per section; a free-play step that fails twice with no smaller stretch to zoom into gets the same lead-in. Forgetting is learnt too (`src/lib/memory.ts`): every bar, hands-together bar and movement has its own half-life, moved after each return following a gap by how much was left (a clean return makes it sturdier), and new things start from his typical half-life; review comes just before it fades (85% left). It suggests stopping when his playing levels off. The meter follows him as he plays: every bar fills from the notes (each hand alone, then both hands) and then the beat, rung by rung, and even a run that did not pass counts for half of what it got right, so it never sits at 0% while he is learning; only the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, on two different days makes 100%. The AI runs on the device: a small prediction model (`src/lib/predict.ts`, a logistic regression), learnt from his own notes, estimates how likely he is to play each possible exercise well and picks the next one near 80-85% (the zone of ideal effort). Exercises are always cut from the song's real notes, never invented: a weak movement gets its own drill, the same move taken from up to four places in the song where he plays it worst (the chord before and the chord it moves to, each kept on its own beat, at least three places, played one after the other), and what he plays there counts for those bars and that move; he never sees the analysis or chooses the exercise, he just gets the next one.
- Each song has a teaching plan (`src/lib/strategy.ts`, shape in `strategyShape.ts`): Claude reads the score (`reading.ts`: every bar's notes per hand, the parts, the repeated shapes, measured density and how often the hands land together) through `api/plan.ts` (Vercel, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) and decides the fastest way in: the order of the parts, real musical phrases, whether each part goes hand by hand per phrase, both hands from the start, or the lead hand through the whole part first, patterns that come back everywhere to learn on their own first, hard spots to drill as soon as their part starts, and how slow to start with the beat. It is asked once per song and version and kept; until it answers, or without a key, rules measured from the notes write the same kind of plan. The plan is checked against the song before it is used, and Mastery follows it while still adapting live to every note he plays. He never sees the plan.
- Hints come from an offline bank, keyed by the kind of mistake (a neighbour key, an overshot leap, a stall before reading, early or late with the beat, hands not together). An LLM may draft the bank; it is reviewed by hand and shipped inside the app. A hint is one short line in the moment after a run, at most one at a time, never interrupting play: only after a run that did not count, never two runs in a row, each kind at most twice a section (`src/lib/hints.ts`: the diagnosis and the bank).
- Finger numbers fade through Mastery: on every note while he learns the notes (a hand alone, both hands, a weak spot), only where the hand moves once he plays the part with the beat, none when he speeds a learnt part up or plays the whole song.
- With the beat, a slip (wrong or missed note) takes him back to beat 1 of the bar before it after a short count-in; past four slips the run stops.
- Accidental touches are never mistakes (`engine.ts`): a wrong key from a keyboard waits 90ms before it counts. A ghost touch (very soft), the key bouncing, a neighbour brushed with the right key, or a key grazed and let go at once is left out (shown as "accidental touches not counted"); several wrong keys at once are one slip.
- The course adapts by topic, not by lesson count (`path.ts`): each unit asks for its skills to reach a goal that rises section by section. Short of it at the unit test, an "Extra practice" lesson made from his weakest of those skills appears (at most three); comfortably past it, the unit's "Personal practice" is not needed. Inside a lesson a perfect free-play run passes the free-play steps it proves (both hands prove each hand) and goes on to the beat; a fast learner starts with both hands and goes back to one at a time if that is hard. No test-outs to skip lessons.
- Free play is the follow mode: the cursor jumps to the next notes and waits, no bars or rests to sit through. Most steps are free; only the last steps of a part or level go "With the beat".
- The MIDI reader writes clean notation: accompaniment chords found in the right-hand track go to the left hand, and slightly short performed notes snap to plain note values.
