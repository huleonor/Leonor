# Leonor: rules for every session

The look is Cartaz (the user's choice in the 2026-11 anti-slop redesign, direction C of four): a Swiss poster, set out in DESIGN.md with its anti-slop rules. The Hallmark skill (Nutlope/hallmark) and the awesome-design-md collection were the sources; DESIGN.md is the system to follow.

Read DESIGN.md before touching any UI. These rules are fixed unless the user changes them.

## Brand (2026-11)
- The app is called Leonor. The logo is a white piano key with its black key forming an "L", flat on the ultramarine field, square, with a thin red now line under it (`public/brand/leonor-mark.svg`; the PNG icons in `public/icons` and the iPhone icon are rendered from it). `<Logo />` shows it. There is no profile screen (the user removed it): the penguin avatar code (`Penguin.tsx`, `Profile.tsx`) stays unused.
- No game elements: no energy, gems, shop, XP, streaks, medals or daily goals, and no Premium or paywall. What is left is the music and his progress in it: a percent per song, parts learnt, and the stars on a practice result. Progress per song shows in the Library rows. The old economy code (`Economy.tsx`, `energy.ts`, `billing.ts`, `Paywall.tsx`, the path) stays in the repo unused for now.
- Tabs: Watch (the feed of the song's phase videos, opening on the next one he has not watched; it is the first screen), Song (the song's map, `Journey.tsx`: a card per phase with its video and practice, a Continue button), Library (his songs; tapping one makes it the song being learnt). Settings under the gear. No profile tab.
- The skill `.claude/skills/mobile-app-ui-design` guides layout and UX; these rules win where they differ (Phosphor not Lucide, no emoji, our tokens and 10px unit).

## Concept (2026-11): one song at a time, taught by reels
- The app teaches one song at a time (`Journey.tsx`, the Song tab), Interstellar Experience by Tony Ann first. The song is cut into its phases, where the music changes (the user's choice: by phase, never by hand, and few of them), and each phase is ONE short vertical video, TikTok style (`Reels.tsx`, `src/lib/reels.ts`), about 40 to 60 seconds, explaining its patterns and tricks like a teacher talking over the keys: a title card with the idea, the phase heard at full speed while the voice explains it, the left hand alone and slow, the right hand alone and slow, then both hands at full speed with captions on the bars that need one, then the tip and "Practise this phase" (the normal lessons of the course parts inside the phase). The Song tab is one card per phase (its video and its practice) and then the whole song.
- A reel is never a recorded video: it is drawn live from the song's own notes (notes falling onto a keyboard per hand, the keys lighting with finger numbers and letters, the bass of each bar boxed, the right hand beat by beat), with the real piano sound, first slowly, then at full speed, then "Your turn". Keys he plays along light green. Swipe up for the next reel; tap to pause. A voice explains it (`src/lib/voice.ts`): the device's own speech engine (Web Speech API, the most natural English voice it has; no recording, no model, no API key) reads the phase's `talk` lines (the idea, each hand, both, the send-off), the piano steps back a little under it, and the caption shows what it says a few words at a time with the word being said lit (from the voice's word marks, or the clock). The timeline gives each line room (`estimate`); there are no full-screen hand-name cards between passes, only a short breath while the notes already fall in. A Voice button beside the reel turns it off; the captions stay.
- The phases and their words (title, hook, tricks, a line for each hand, captions by bar, a tip, and what the voice says, `talk`) are written by hand, by Claude in a session, after reading the score bar by bar, and shipped in `src/lib/reelScripts.ts` (limits and checks in `reelShape.ts`); no model and no API key at runtime. A script fits a song by the reading's hash (`version`, for the MIDI files shipped with the app) or, for a song he brings as his own MIDI, by the bass at fingerprint bars of the printed score (70% must match). A song without a script, or notes that do not match, gets its course parts grouped into at most seven phases and words the rules write from the notes. To write one, `await window.__reelFacts('song-id')` in the dev console gives every bar's bass and right hand and the score as text. Note letters follow the key (sharps, flats in a flat key). Scripts exist for Gymnopédie No. 1 (6 phases), Für Elise (2) and Interstellar Experience (7, read from the MuseScore score: 82 bars, 4/4, crotchet 99, two bars of 3/4 at 57-58). Never turn a copyrighted score into a MIDI file for him: he brings the MIDI. Repairing the MIDI he brings is fine (re-barring a converter's timing, dropping clef or time-signature signs it read as notes, putting a misread clef back, applying octave lines it ignored, reusing a bar of his own file where the score repeats it), and so is filling the few bars a converter lost, read from his own copy of the score, when he asks; transcribing a whole score is not. The repair script lives in the session scratchpad, never in the repo. His repaired Interstellar Experience file never goes in the repo.
- A hand's stretch in a phase video starts on the first bar where that hand plays.
- Time-signature changes are kept (`song.meter`, read from the MIDI): every bar's start and length come from the bar grid (`src/lib/bars.ts`), used by the parts, reels, reading, Mastery, the engine's bar lines and count-in, the metronome (each bar ticks in its own metre, beat 1 accented) and the score (an inline `[M:3/4]` where the metre changes). Never compute a bar as `ppq * 4 * num / den` from `song.timeSig` alone.
- Interstellar Experience is under copyright: it is a slot that takes his own MIDI file, like the other film and modern pieces; nothing of it ships with the app.

## Design system (Cartaz, a Swiss poster; DESIGN.md has the detail)
- Colours only through the tokens in `src/styles.css` `:root`. Never hardcode a colour in a component or rule; add a token. A canvas reads the tokens with `getComputedStyle` on its own element. There is one theme, light.
- Paper ground (`--paper`, wells `--paper-2`), blue-black ink (`--ink`, `--ink-2`; `--muted` only for hints and labels), hairline rules (`--rule`). One ultramarine field (`--field`, paper text, `--field-muted`, `--field-rule`) where the music is: the reels, the falling-notes view, the next phase on the Song page, the song learnt now in the Library, a chosen option. One red (`--mark`) for the left hand and a wrong note; one green (`--ok`) for a right note or a good run. No gradients, glows, shadows or glass.
- Hands: on paper the right hand is the field blue and the left the red; on the field the right hand prints in paper (lit keys `--right-key`) and the left stays red.
- One primary style: ink fill with paper text (on the field, paper fill with field text). Default buttons are paper-2 cells with a hairline; quiet ones are underlined words. Sentence case, never uppercase, never tracked.
- Two families: Radio Canada Big 700 for heads and every big number (tracking -0.035em, line-height about 0.95), Radio Canada 400/500 for words at 16px. Hierarchy by size; numbers set big (the bar on a reel, the phase number, the percent).
- Square corners everywhere; circles only for small dots. Structure from rows and hairlines, not cards.
- Spacing on a 10px unit (10, 20, 30, 40).
- Motion is state-driven (`src/components/Motion.tsx`, the `motion` library): every animation answers a change of state, never decoration (a bar number sliding in, a percent bumping, a verdict popping, sheets rising, the help keyboard rising and falling). CSS transitions 300ms ease for colours. No loops, no confetti. The app runs inside `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">`.
- Focus: 2px solid field outline, 2px offset.

## Product UI rules (from the anti-slop skill)
- Icons: Phosphor only (`@phosphor-icons/react`), bold weight, fill for solid marks. Never hand-draw SVG icon paths; add the glyph to `Icons.tsx` or `GameIcons.tsx`.
- Zero em dashes and en dashes in anything the user sees. Use a hyphen, comma, colon or two sentences.
- Body text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1) on its ground. Paper text on the red or the green only at 15px and up or in display type.
- Screenshot phone and desktop before committing UI changes.
- No gradients, no purple, no decorative dots, no emoji, no middle dots joining text (a comma or a full stop instead), no eyebrows in capitals.
- One verb per action across the app. Button labels stay on one line.
- Loading, empty and error states for anything that loads.
- The logo is Leonor's key mark (`<Logo />`, see Brand). The penguin avatar (`Penguin.tsx`, drawn from plain circles and ellipses in Duolingo's geometric style, colours from the `--av-*` tokens): he picks its background, feathers and what it wears (glasses, headphones, beanie, bow tie, crown) on his profile, and it is the Profile tab's mark. No other characters or mascots and no icon-on-a-disc illustrations: the picture of a song is its own opening on a staff (`<OpeningStaff />`, `SongCover.tsx`). Never use Duolingo's owl, logo or name in the app.
- The difficulty adapts invisibly: never show levels or "made easier" notes to the learner. Songs show no difficulty either.
- Step-by-step lessons (path lessons and a song's parts) are Simply Piano's page, the user's choice: a white page (`--white`), the staff big across it (the grand staff about half the stage's height; a short step grows to fill the width, a long one slides, never less than a bar on screen), a blue now line, the help keyboard a white strip along the bottom edge: the bar holds only pause (left) and the n/N step pill with its arrows and rep dots (right), sized like Simply's (44px pause, 34px counter and arrows, all square); no step name, chips or piano icon on it (the step name is for screen readers). The staff is Simply Piano's: its lines run on across the whole screen, the clef and key signature stay at the left edge, played notes fade out behind them, and it grows with the screen. Nothing moves before he plays: the cursor starts on the first note, no run-up.
- The keyboard is help so he learns to read: never there from the start (the coach can never set it to "right away"; a stall counts only after his first note), hidden until he plays a wrong note or stalls, then a compact strip with every key the piece needs (next key yellow, `--key-next`) rises along the bottom and sinks again once he plays right. Options can keep it on.
- Every level teaches Simply Piano's way: free play first (the music waits for each note, no rhythm; hand by hand for two hands), then the same music "With the beat" with the metronome ticking (count-in, accent on beat 1). The metronome can be switched off in the options.
- Playing a whole song is minimal: close, progress bar, the score on a card, Listen, Learn step by step and Mastery below, options under a gear. There the keyboard is help, not furniture: it rises only when he is stuck or plays a wrong note, shows a short stretch of keys with the right ones lit, and goes away again. "Play the first note to start" shows until he does.
- Life comes from state: praise popping at milestones (5, 10, 15, 20, 30 right in a row), the step pill springing forward, a step's verdict popping green or red. No streak chip.
- The piano connection is never a banner: a small piano-keys icon with a red dot (green when connected) in the game bar opens Settings. Lessons do not show it: setting up the keyboard lives in Settings.
- Tabs are words (Watch, Song, Library, Settings), no icons; a 3px ink bar marks the current one.
- The old unit path (`PathHome.tsx`, `path.ts`) is no longer a tab: Learn is the song's journey. Its code stays for now. When it was shown: the path had life, Duolingo style but without characters: every unit has a picture beside the trail (a big Phosphor glyph on a tinted disc in the unit's colour, with notes around it, `PathArt.tsx`), grey until the unit opens; nodes ahead keep their own icon in grey (`--locked-ink`) instead of a lock; finished lessons show their stars under the node. Tapping the unit banner (or a section divider) opens the Sections page (`Sections.tsx`): a card per section with its picture on a staff, what it teaches, units done, and Continue or Review; later sections show locked. No jumping ahead.
- Mastery checks (`mastery.ts`): after 100% a check falls due after a week, then two weeks, then a month: the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, any day from the due date, with a week's grace. When one is due it is the next Mastery section. Three kept and the song is Forever (no more checks). A check missed past the grace drops it to silver until he plays it clean again, which brings gold back and starts the checks over; nothing is lost for good.
- Song covers (`SongCover.tsx`) are one design for every piece, never photos: a tile in one of the `--cover-*` colours with a staff across it carrying the song's own opening (the first notes of its melody, in white, at their pitch); a big white Phosphor glyph stands in when the notes are not loaded (a slot waiting for its MIDI). The app's songs take the colours in turn; an imported MIDI gets one by hash.
- Songs: public-domain pieces ship with the app (Gymnopédie No. 1, the opening of Für Elise written by `scripts/fur-elise.mjs`, Handel's Sarabande and Passacaglia, Clair de Lune); Gymnopédie is in the library from the start. Film and modern pieces under copyright are slots in the marketplace that take his own MIDI file. The Songs tab shows only his library: the cover, title, composer and a Mastery bar with its percent (or the length and "not started"). The marketplace shows each piece with its composer, work and year, a line on what it is like, and its length, key and hands (or "Bring your own MIDI file").
- Mastery plays like Simply Piano: a section (a part of the song) is a run of steps, phrase by phrase (about four bars, never under two), each repeated until it has its good runs (the dots under the step count turn into green ticks; once in free play, twice with the beat), so he keeps moving through the song. A part is learnt enough to go on to the next once he has played it with the beat, clean at any speed, nearly right, or twice and more than half right; the song keeps moving and what is still weak comes back later (each part faster, warm-ups, drills), so he feels it growing. After each run only a tick or "again" shows, then the same step, the next one, or a smaller stretch where it went wrong: no screens and no taps. With the beat the count-in starts by itself. The result card shows only at the end of a section, for three seconds, then the next section starts on its own (unless it suggests stopping for the day). The lesson staff is drawn like Simply's: a white page (`--staff-ground`), thin grey staff and bar lines (`--staff-line`), the two staves one staff-height apart (`%%sysstaffsep 20`), black notes, green once played, red when wrong, finger numbers in bold Radio Canada Big that stay black (above the note for the right hand, below it for the left), no time signature, and no cursor in free play (the now line shows only with the beat); about four bars across and as big as the screen's height allows. A step that fits on the screen stays still and centred and the now line moves over it; a longer one slides under the now line.
- Mastery (`src/lib/mastery.ts`) plans a song's lessons live from every note he plays: a strength per bar, per hand, per hands-together and per movement (leaps, jumps to a chord, chord changes, new positions). Notes first in free play, then a tempo ladder (60% to 100%) climbed after two good runs; a good run is 90% right with at most one slip. A stretch that goes wrong twice in one place gets that place (two bars, one hand if only one slipped) put in front of it; three misses with the beat slow it down (two when the model gives another try under 50%); four and it is left for another day. A step the model gives under 60% gets one lead-in played once just before it (the same music a rung slower, its harder half, or the harder hand alone, whichever is closest to the target and at least 75%), at most two per section; a free-play step that fails twice with no smaller stretch to zoom into gets the same lead-in. Forgetting is learnt too (`src/lib/memory.ts`): every bar, hands-together bar and movement has its own half-life, moved after each return following a gap by how much was left (a clean return makes it sturdier), and new things start from his typical half-life; review comes just before it fades (85% left). It suggests stopping when his playing levels off. The meter follows him as he plays: every bar fills from the notes (each hand alone, then both hands) and then the beat, rung by rung, and even a run that did not pass counts for half of what it got right, so it never sits at 0% while he is learning; only the whole song with the beat at full speed, 95% right, on two different days makes 100%. The AI runs on the device: a small prediction model (`src/lib/predict.ts`, a logistic regression), learnt from his own notes, estimates how likely he is to play each possible exercise well and picks the next one near 80-85% (the zone of ideal effort). Exercises are always cut from the song's real notes, never invented: a weak movement gets its own drill, the same move taken from up to four places in the song where he plays it worst (the chord before and the chord it moves to, each kept on its own beat, at least three places, played one after the other), and what he plays there counts for those bars and that move; he never sees the analysis or chooses the exercise, he just gets the next one.
- Each song has a teaching plan (`src/lib/strategy.ts`, shape in `strategyShape.ts`): Claude reads the score (`reading.ts`: every bar's notes per hand, the parts, the repeated shapes, measured density and how often the hands land together) through `api/plan.ts` (Vercel, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; `GROQ_API_KEY` for testing, its plans asked again each time the app opens) and decides the fastest way in: the order of the parts, real musical phrases, whether each part goes hand by hand per phrase, both hands from the start, or the lead hand through the whole part first, patterns that come back everywhere to learn on their own first, hard spots to drill as soon as their part starts, and how slow to start with the beat. It is asked once per song and version and kept; until it answers, or without a key, rules measured from the notes write the same kind of plan. The plan is checked against the song before it is used, and Mastery follows it while still adapting live to every note he plays. He never sees the plan.
- Hints come from an offline bank, keyed by the kind of mistake (a neighbour key, an overshot leap, a stall before reading, early or late with the beat, hands not together). An LLM may draft the bank; it is reviewed by hand and shipped inside the app. A hint is one short line in the moment after a run, at most one at a time, never interrupting play: only after a run that did not count, never two runs in a row, each kind at most twice a section (`src/lib/hints.ts`: the diagnosis and the bank).
- Finger numbers fade through Mastery: on every note while he learns the notes (a hand alone, both hands, a weak spot), only where the hand moves once he plays the part with the beat, none when he speeds a learnt part up or plays the whole song.
- With the beat in Mastery, while he is new to it (60% and 70%, and less at 80%), the music waits a moment at a note he has not played (1.5 s, 1 s, 0.5 s) instead of marking it missed, and a slip never sends him back; from 90%, and in the whole song, reviews and checks, it is played for real.
- With the beat, every count-in is a bar in tempo (1, 2, 3 in 3/4, ticking on the beat) and the music starts on beat 1 right after it, so he feels where the first note lands. A note counts from 0.3 s early until the now line has passed it (half its length, 0.3 to 0.6 s late); a note played on the last count is on time, and the right key a little outside that window is not a wrong note. A slip (wrong or missed note) takes him back to beat 1 of the bar before it, after the same count-in; past four slips the run stops.
- Accidental touches are never mistakes (`engine.ts`): a wrong key from a keyboard waits 90ms before it counts. A ghost touch (very soft), the key bouncing, a neighbour brushed with the right key, or a key grazed and let go at once is left out (shown as "accidental touches not counted"); several wrong keys at once are one slip.
- The course adapts by topic, not by lesson count (`path.ts`): each unit asks for its skills to reach a goal that rises section by section. Short of it at the unit test, an "Extra practice" lesson made from his weakest of those skills appears (at most three); comfortably past it, the unit's "Personal practice" is not needed. Inside a lesson a perfect free-play run passes the free-play steps it proves (both hands prove each hand) and goes on to the beat; a fast learner starts with both hands and goes back to one at a time if that is hard. No test-outs to skip lessons.
- Free play is the follow mode: the cursor jumps to the next notes and waits, no bars or rests to sit through. Most steps are free; only the last steps of a part or level go "With the beat".
- The MIDI reader writes clean notation: accompaniment chords found in the right-hand track go to the left hand, and slightly short performed notes snap to plain note values.
